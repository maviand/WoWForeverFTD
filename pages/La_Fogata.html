01 — What you're actually walking into

World of Warcraft: Forever, in brief

Announced September 12, 2026 at BlizzCon, WoW: Forever is Blizzard's official "Classic+" — vanilla Azeroth (level cap still 60) kept intact but built forward with new zones, dungeons, and systems instead of being left to age as a museum piece. It's included with an existing WoW subscription, so nobody in the group needs to buy anything extra.

New ground

Four new zones (Mount Hyjal, the Riverglades, Shen'dralas, Zephras Isle), nine new dungeons, two new max-level raids at launch, and 1,000 new quests spread across the 1–60 bracket.

New characters

A new neutral race, the Skyborne, plus new race/class pairings — including some that break vanilla's old faction locks. Full breakdown below.

Itemization rework

Hit and crit are unified across spell/melee/ranged, weapon skill is trimmed off most gear, and hybrid specs get real support instead of leftover stats. Full class-by-class detail in the Classes section.

Two new systems

Legacy (account-wide progression across alts) and Campfire (a placeable social/crafting hub). Both covered in full further down.

Why this matters before you even log in

Nothing here is a traditional realm list. Forever is realmless — you pick a ruleset once, and it quietly decides your world PvP exposure, who you can group with, and even how names work. Get that choice right first; everything else in this guide builds on it.

02 — Foundation

Ruleset: Normal

Forever splits its population into four separate, non-interoperable ecosystems — Normal, PvP, Roleplaying, and Hardcore — each with its own economy, auction house, and mail. You cannot group across them, and Horde can't group with Alliance in any of them. This is the first decision your ten make, and it has to be unanimous before anyone reserves a name.

Recommendation: Normal. Open-world PvP requires mutual consent, so your guild spends its time questing, professions-grinding, and raiding together instead of getting camped mid-quest by an opposing guild that outgears you. Given the group's stated goal — casual, social-first, eventually scaling to 20-player raids — Normal removes the single biggest source of unplanned friction. Save PvP-ruleset ambitions for later; nothing stops a curious member from rolling a Hardcore alt on the side once the main guild is established (see Extras).

Two setup details worth planning around now

Two-part names. Characters use a first-and-last-name format, unique per region rather than per realm. Decide your naming convention as a group before Name Reservation Day (October 27) — a shared theme reads well later and avoids ten people scrambling for the same popular name that morning.

Language preference. Forever matches players by a selected language preference within a ruleset. Since your core ten are Dominican and likely play primarily in Spanish, set that preference explicitly during account setup — it shapes who you run into while pugging group content before your own twenty is full, and it's independent of faction, so it doesn't narrow the choice below.

03 — Identity

Faction: Horde or Alliance — two rosters, your call

The old logic — "Alliance has Paladin, Horde has Shaman, pick your hybrid" — no longer applies. Forever adds six new race/class combinations, three per side: Horde gains Orc Mage, Troll Warlock, and Undead Paladin; Alliance gains Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, and Dwarf Shaman. Both factions now field every class, and Forever explicitly supports Horde/Alliance population equilibrium the way Season of Discovery did — so "which side is more populated," historically a real vanilla concern, isn't a factor either. Below are two independent suggested 10-person spreads, one per faction, so you can pick on vibe and lore rather than mechanics.

04 — Composition

Roles and the road from 10 to 20

Tier 1 raid content unlocks three sizes simultaneously on December 9, 2026: Barrow Deeps (10-player), Hyjal Summit (20-player), and Onyxia's Lair (40-player). Your stated plan — start at 10, grow to 20 — lines up exactly with the first two. Treat Onyxia's Lair as a stretch goal reached by allying with another guild for one night, not something to build your roster around from day one.

Warrior Tank / Melee DPS

Protection gets the deep dive — see the tank comparison above first.
Baseline (all specs)

Victory Rush Corroborated — a new post-kill attack/heal, exact values still shifting. The old shared cooldown across Recklessness, Retaliation, and Shield Wall Corroborated is gone or greatly shortened, so panic-button cooldowns are far more available than vanilla. Unified Hit improves Taunt reliability, and weapon-skill gear is deliberately devalued so it's no longer a mandatory itemization trap. Weaponmaster Corroborated consolidates the four separate Axe/Sword/Mace/Polearm Specialization talents into one, and a new stat reduces enemy dodge/parry chance against your attacks.

Arms DPS

Spearing Strike Corroborated (new: 15 Rage, 40% weapon damage, bonus vs. Giants/Dragonkin, dismounts mounted targets). Bloodthrill Single-source — melee attacks against a Rend-afflicted target can proc a free, guaranteed Overpower. Improved Slam drops to a 2-point talent and finally removes the old "Slam interrupts your swing timer" downside that made it unpopular in vanilla.

Fury DPS

Dual Wield Specialization expands to grant off-hand hit chance and rage generation, not just damage. Raging Blows Corroborated makes Whirlwind also strike with your off-hand and cheapens Cleave. Enrage is reworked to trigger off any damage taken or dealt, not just crits, at a smaller magnitude Single-source. One source flags Bloodthirst's healing possibly shifting to a movement-speed buff with a new talent, Blood Craze, taking over sustain Speculative — treat as unconfirmed.

Protection Tank — the deep-dive spec

Strong agreement across sources on direction: a more "pure" shield-tank identity with real defensive tools, and enough stance flexibility that you spend less time stance-dancing. Charge and Thunder Clap become usable in Defensive Stance Corroborated via a new talent, Vanguard. Most Protection talents now explicitly require a shield equipped, closing off any dual-wield "tank" builds for good. New talents: Bastion (damage while shielded), Master of Defense (rage on dodge/parry with a shield up), Focused Rage, and Vitality. Defiance is reworked to scale all threat generated in Defensive Stance. Shield Slam is buffed with block-value scaling and a 50% chance to dispel a magic effect, and is explicitly called out as very high threat. Shield Wall and Last Stand come up more often than vanilla per multiple sources.

Itemization notes

Unified Hit specifically improves Taunt reliability, since Taunt rolled against melee hit separately in vanilla. New parry/dodge-reduction gear stats directly help tank threat and melee uptime.

Druid Tank / DPS / Healer

Feral gets the deep dive — see the tank comparison above first.
Baseline (all specs)

Revive Corroborated — a new out-of-combat resurrection spell, distinct from Rebirth. Omen of Clarity becomes baseline and expands to proc off offensive spells and heals, not just melee swings. Furor is reworked specifically to kill the old vanilla "powershifting" exploit macro: shifting into Cat now grants scaled Energy, shifting into Bear grants a flat Rage amount, instead of a full resource refill. Cat and Bear Form attacks now scale with your equipped weapon's damage Single-source — a real new gearing axis (see the tank comparison above). Improved Mark of the Wild is baseline Official, freeing a talent point every Druid used to spend on raid buff coverage.

Balance DPS

The headline change is a new Eclipse-style system Corroborated: casting Wrath reduces your next Starfire cast time, stacking up to 4 charges — importing the alternating Wrath/Starfire rotation vanilla Balance never had. Nature's Grace is reworked into a flat casting-speed buff on any non-periodic crit. Moonkin Form's aura is broadened to buff more spell/ability types for nearby party members, not just caster crit.

Feral Bear Tank / Cat DPS — the deep-dive spec

Tiger's Fury flips from an Energy cost to an Energy grant plus a damage buff Corroborated. Berserk (new, shared Cat/Bear cooldown, ~3 min / 15 sec): in Bear Form removes Mangle's cooldown and lets it hit up to 3 targets; in Cat Form grants guaranteed crits to combo-point builders; grants fear immunity while active. For the tank side specifically: Mangle is added as a core Bear ability with no dodge/parry requirement Corroborated, and Lacerate adds a stacking bleed (up to 5 stacks) for sustained threat. Natural Reaction converts dodges into rage. Thick Hide now also scales off Defense skill, making that stat worth itemizing for the first time. Frenzied Regeneration is reported as a substantially stronger self-heal than vanilla's version Single-source. Bleeds and DoTs (Rake, Rip, Lacerate) can now crit.

Restoration Healer

Wild Growth Single-source — a new party-wide heal-over-time. Swiftmend is reworked so it no longer consumes the HoT it's cast on Corroborated — a real rotational change from vanilla's "Swiftmend eats your HoT" tradeoff. Gift of the Earthmother Corroborated, matching numbers across sources — cuts the GCD on Rejuvenation, Swiftmend, and Wild Growth by half a second. HoTs can now crit.

Paladin Tank / DPS / Healer

The one class with a full official Blizzard deep dive.
Baseline (all specs) Official

Holy Strike — new at level 6: an instant weapon strike dealing Holy damage, 12-second cooldown. Seals are no longer consumed by Judgment — a foundational rotational change from vanilla. Seal of Fury favors fast weapons, grants an absorb shield, and causes Judgment to taunt. Consecration is baseline at level 20, dealing high damage/threat to the first four targets hit. Design limits are explicitly preserved: Paladins still lack a real interrupt, have limited slowing tools, and rely on Seal/Judgment for taunting — they remain especially strong against Undead and Demons.

Holy Healer Official

Improved Holy Strike (cooldown reduction), Voice of Truth (Silence/Interrupt immunity), Reverence (mana regen from Spirit while casting), Infusion of Light (reduces Holy Light cast time after a Holy Shock or Flash of Light crit), Holy Shock moved to a 10-second cooldown, Consecrated Ground (bonus Holy damage inside your Consecration), and Light's Vigil — a high-cost cooldown that resets Holy Shock or triggers party-wide damage/healing.

Protection Tank Official

Improved Seal of Fury (mana on absorb), Shield Specialization (mana on block), Swift Judgment (resets Judgment's cooldown once per minute), Templar's Bulwark (absorb shield scaling off max health), Reckoning redesigned around crit-immune tanking and triggering off blocks, and Iron Creed — timed Holy Strikes reduce incoming damage.

Retribution DPS Official — biggest itemization winner

Vindication (reduces enemy Attack Power; since the Oct 30 hotfix it no longer raises your own), Sacred Arbiter (empowers Holy Strike, refreshes Judgments), Champion of the Light (spell damage scaling off Intellect, up to 60% of it after the Oct 30 rework), Instrument of the Law (reduces threat, makes Holy Wrath instant; removed in the Oct 30 hotfix), Twist of Light (Seal Twisting without needing an addon), and a Holy Shock option for a ranged, spell-powered playstyle. This spec is Blizzard's own named example of who benefits most from the unified hit/crit system.

Hunter Ranged / Melee DPS

Survival becomes a genuine melee spec.
Baseline (all specs)

Unified Hit improves Trap reliability — traps rolled against a separate, easy-to-overlook hit check in vanilla. Aspect of the Beast now also grants melee Attack Power. Vanilla's ranged "dead zone" and the original Disengage are explicitly kept rather than modernized — confirmation that Hunter isn't being globally retail-ified. New combos: Human Hunter and Skyborne Hunter (both factions).

Beast Mastery DPS

Summon Hawk Corroborated, matching numbers across sources — summons a hawk that dive-bombs the target for 18 seconds, up to 2 active at once, sharing a 6-second cooldown with Arcane Shot. Effectively a second pet stacked on top of your main one. Focused Fire reworked to grant flat damage per point while your pet is actively out.

Marksmanship DPS

Lone Wolf Corroborated, consistent number — +20% damage to all attacks while you have no active pet, explicitly positioning MM as the "petless" Hunter spec against BM's pet-uptime focus. Careful Aim reworked to convert 20% of Intellect into Attack Power Corroborated — one of the better-confirmed numbers in the whole Hunter kit. Sniper Shot (new cast-time nuke) rounds out the tree.

Survival Melee DPS — repositioned

Every source agrees on the direction: Survival becomes Hunter's dedicated melee spec, a real identity shift from vanilla's ranged/trap hybrid. Strider Kick (new melee attack) and Lacerating Strikes Corroborated, matching numbers — Mongoose Bite applies a bleed worth 40% of the bite's damage over 21 seconds. Entrapment makes your traps also root the target briefly, guaranteeing a follow-up window.

Rogue Melee DPS

Mutilate arrives; Combat gets a cooldown-refund identity.
Baseline (all specs)

Sap no longer breaks Stealth Corroborated. Rogues can now equip one-handed Axes. The unified Hit/Crit system explicitly extends to Poison application chance and Poison crit, not just weapon attacks — a real buff to poison-based builds. Armor Penetration still only applies to armor-mitigated hits, not poison or bleed damage over time.

Assassination DPS

Mutilate Corroborated — the later-expansion signature ability arrives in Forever: both weapons for 75% weapon damage plus a flat bonus, 2 combo points, +20% against Poisoned targets. Venom (new finisher) boosts poison damage and application chance for a duration scaling with combo points spent — reframing the spec around poison uptime rather than pure burst.

Combat DPS

Restless Blades (new) — damaging finishing moves reduce the cooldown of Adrenaline Rush, Blade Flurry, Evasion, Sprint, and Vanish by 2 seconds per combo point spent, a real cooldown-management redesign. Hack and Slash (new) consolidates all five weapon-specialization talents into one, with the bonus depending on weapon type equipped.

Subtlety DPS

Hemorrhage becomes a core baseline ability Corroborated — instant strike, applies a personal debuff (+15% Rupture damage from you specifically, not all sources) rather than vanilla's generic damage-taken debuff. Thousand Cuts (new) cheapens your next builder each time Rupture ticks. Cutthroat (new) gives Backstab a chance to let your next Ambush skip the Stealth requirement.

Priest Healer / DPS

Prayer of Mending arrives; Shadowform gets a real identity shift.
Baseline (all specs)

Divine Spirit moves out of the talent tree and becomes baseline utility, freeing a point every Holy/Disc Priest used to spend on it. Devouring Plague (previously Undead-only) and Fear Ward (previously Dwarf-only) open up to all Priests. Gnome joins as a new playable Priest race.

Discipline Healer

Penance (new signature spell) — a channeled bolt dealing damage or healing, firing repeatedly over a short channel, 12-second cooldown; a direct retrofit of the ability's later-expansion identity. Divine Aegis (new) — critical heals generate a protective absorb shield. Soul Warding cuts Power Word: Shield's cooldown and cost.

Holy Healer

Prayer of Mending (new) — a major new mechanic: a heal-on-next-damage buff that jumps between party members up to 5 times within a window, rather than a single-target spell. Litany of Light refunds a portion of mana on a different heal when you rotate between spells instead of spamming one. Binding Heal (new) heals both target and caster at once, at reduced threat. Spirit of Redemption's duration extends from 10 to 15 seconds.

Shadow DPS

Shadowform reworked Corroborated — damage bonus trimmed from 15% to 10%, but gains a new 50% mana-cost reduction on Shadow spells and doubled critical damage, while the healing-spell restriction while shapeshifted is tightened. Vampiric Embrace now has its own cooldown and triggers Spirit Tap on a kill. Shadow Weaving flips from an enemy-facing debuff into a personal stacking damage buff. DoTs can now crit, system-wide — especially relevant here.

Shaman DPS / Healer

Maelstrom Weapon and Riptide arrive; Dwarf Shaman is live.
Baseline (all specs)

Two-Handed Axes and Maces usable without spending a talent point. Lightning Bolt's cast time drops from 3.0 to 2.5 seconds. Totemic Projection/Recall (new) let you reposition dropped totems without redropping them. Call of the Elements (new) drops a full totem set in one cast. Fire Nova is reworked from its own totem into a detonation of your active Fire Totem. The unified Hit/Crit merge is explicitly named as a top beneficiary for Enhancement's mixed melee-and-spell kit, alongside Retribution Paladin.

Elemental DPS

Lava Burst (new nuke) deals +20% damage if the target already has your Flame Shock on it — a DoT-into-nuke synergy vanilla Elemental never had. Call of Flame broadens beyond Fire Totem to also buff Flame Shock, Fire Nova, and Lava Burst. Lightning Overload (new) gives Nature spells a chance to duplicate their effect at no extra threat — an early version of a mechanic later expansions built whole trees around.

Enhancement Hybrid DPS — biggest itemization winner

Maelstrom Weapon (new signature talent) — melee attacks stack a buff (up to 5) that cuts Lightning Bolt's cast time and mana cost, turning Enhancement into a melee-into-instant-caster hybrid rotation vanilla never had. Stormstrike reworked into a personal +20% Nature damage buff with a much shorter cooldown. Spirit Weapons (new) grants Parry and can flip from reducing threat to increasing it, letting Enhancement flex into an off-tank role in a pinch.

Restoration Healer

Riptide (new signature spell) combines a direct heal and a heal-over-time in one cast, and boosts a follow-up Chain Heal on the same target — Restoration's new capstone tool. Water Shield (new, returning) — a 3-charge shield restoring mana when you're hit or land a healing crit. Tidal Focus reworked to boost your global Hit chance alongside its existing cost reduction.

Mage Ranged DPS

Arcane gets the biggest rework of the three specs.
Baseline (all specs)

Frostfire Bolt exists as a real, talentable hybrid Fire/Frost spell. Improved Counterspell is reworked so rank 1 guarantees a 2-second silence outright, instead of vanilla's chance-based version. Orc Mage and Skyborne Mage (Alliance) are new combos.

Arcane DPS — most reworked spec

Described by multiple sources as going from largely unplayed in vanilla to genuinely strong. Arcane Blast (new) stacks a self-buff on cast that boosts your other spell damage per stack, at a rising mana cost — a direct retrofit of the ability's later, more iconic identity. Missile Barrage (new) gives your casts a proc chance to cut Arcane Missiles' channel time in half and remove its mana cost entirely. Arcane Meditation's mana regen while casting jumps substantially, addressing Arcane's traditionally poor sustained-casting economy.

Fire DPS

Hot Streak Corroborated — Fire's new signature mechanic: non-periodic crits from Fireball, Frostfire Bolt, Fire Blast, or Scorch stack up to 3 times, each stack cutting Pyroblast's cast time by 25%. This directly imports Fire's most iconic later-expansion mechanic into the vanilla-style kit. Combustion is reworked to end after 4 Fire crits instead of 3.

Frost DPS

Ice Lance (new) — instant cast, weak against normal targets but roughly +300% damage against Frozen ones. Fingers of Frost (new) gives Chill effects a chance to make your next spell treat the target as Frozen even if it isn't literally frozen, enabling Ice Lance/Shatter synergy on demand. Shatter now only costs 3 talent points to max instead of 5 — a real investment reduction for Frost's core burst talent. Since the Oct 28 build Ice Lance also has an 8-second cooldown.

Warlock Ranged DPS

The most extensively reworked class in the whole game, per multiple sources.
Baseline (all specs)

DoTs can now critically strike Corroborated — described repeatedly as the single biggest baseline change to the class, with knock-on effects across every Warlock spec. Bane of Agony and Bane of Doom replace the old Curse of Agony/Doom and no longer compete for your one curse slot — you can run a Bane and a separate curse on the same target at once. Warlock pets now scale with your own stats, addressing vanilla's well-known problem of pets falling off badly at higher gear levels. Troll Warlock is a new combo.

Affliction DPS — rebuilt around Drain amplification

Multiple sources describe the spec shifting from Shadow Bolt spam toward maintaining multiple DoTs and amplifying them with Drain spells. Drain Hope (new capstone) is a channeled Shadow DoT that also buffs your other Shadow DoTs on the same target. Improved Drains boosts Drain Life/Soul damage per active DoT on the target, with the Drain Soul portion tripled below 20% target health — explicitly framed as giving Affliction a real execute phase for the first time.

Demonology DPS — built around pet uptime

Demonic Pact (new capstone) lets your Demonic Sacrifice buff persist even after summoning a different demon — no more choosing between "keep the buff" and "have a pet out." Demonic Sacrifice is reworked with reshuffled per-demon buffs and its duration extended from 30 minutes to 2 hours. Demonic Knowledge boosts your own spell damage and healing while a demon is active, tying personal output directly to pet uptime.

Destruction DPS — stays a hard-cast spec on purpose

Sources agree Destruction is being redesigned to blend Fire and Shadow damage together while deliberately keeping vanilla's slower, hard-cast identity rather than becoming proc-heavy. Incinerate Corroborated (new capstone nuke) deals bonus damage against Immolate-afflicted targets. Shadow and Flame (new) ties Conflagrate to Shadow damage and Shadowburn to Fire damage as a cross-buff. Bane of Havoc (new) copies a portion of damage you deal to other targets onto a Baned target — a real two-target cleave tool the spec never had.

Cross-class itemization confirmed by Blizzard alongside the unified hit/crit merge: weapon skill is deliberately devalued on gear; healing gear now also grants roughly a third as much bonus spell damage, so healers can solo-quest without a separate damage set; spellcaster weapons carry spell power/healing directly, down to low levels; trinkets are becoming more situational rather than flat always-on stats; and some gear now ties bonus effects to specific environments or creature types.

06 — Economy

Professions strategy

Forever adds over 600 new recipes across every profession and level range, plus Advanced Blueprint recipes that drop specifically from dungeon bosses — a real reason to run your own nine new dungeons instead of skipping straight to max level. The bigger structural change, though, is that professions now feed directly into the Campfire system (next section): each profession contributes a craftable buff-item to any campsite, so a guild with good profession coverage functions like a mobile buff station on every leveling and farming trip.

07 — New mechanics

Legacy and Campfire, explained

Legacy: your account's long game

Legacy is account-wide progression earned through Legacy Challenges, spent independently on each character across three trees: Adventure (rested-XP and leveling perks), Professions (gathering yield, like Bountiful Harvest), and Resourcefulness (economy perks like the Reagent Economy above). You start with up to 65 points to spend and a 16-point-per-character cap at launch, specifically so alts don't feel mandatory just to stay competitive.

Practical read for your guild: Legacy Points earned on anyone's main help fund that same player's future alts, but they don't help a guildmate directly. It's a personal system — plan your own character's tree, don't expect to trade progress with anyone else.

Campfire: your mobile guild hall

Any character can drop a Basic Campfire (learned through Cooking, available to everyone as a secondary skill) to create a campsite offering repairs, a vendor, and one-hour buffs. Up to three nearby players can each contribute one profession-crafted item to the site — expandable to ten contributions at higher skill levels — turning a rest stop into a real gathering point.

Worth flagging even on Normal ruleset

Campsites are not sanctuaries. On Normal you're safe from unwanted PvP by default, but don't assume a campsite is special protection on top of that — pitch camp somewhere sensible, same as you would any other open-world stop.

For your guild specifically: treat the Campfire as the natural default meeting spot before a dungeon run or a group leveling session — it's a small ritual (drop camp, buff up, head out together) that costs nothing and reinforces exactly the social, casual tone your group already chose.

08 — Structure

Guild structure for a casual, social-first crew

You chose casual/social first over progression-focused, which means the document's job here is to add just enough structure to prevent friction — not to build a raiding bureaucracy nobody asked for.

Leadership

One Guild Master (whoever's most likely to actually keep logging in for admin tasks) plus two Officers — one owns Discord/recruiting, one owns raid-night logistics. Rotate if it stops being fun for whoever holds it.

Loot

Need/Greed for dungeon drops, simple round-robin soft-reserve for raid drops. No DKP spreadsheet — it's overhead a ten-to-twenty friend guild doesn't need at this ambition level.

Attendance

No formal policy. A pinned Discord poll before each raid night tells you who's coming; if under threshold, it becomes a dungeon or leveling night instead. Nobody owes an explanation for missing.

Guild bank

One shared bank tab for BoE crafting mats and campfire reagents everyone can pull from. Keep gold in personal banks — a shared gold pool is the single most common source of casual-guild drama.

11 — Growth

Getting from 10 to 20: vetted friends-of-friends

  1. Each core member may nominate up to 2 people they'd personally vouch for — this naturally caps the search and keeps a line of accountability back to someone already in the guild.
  2. A short, informal trial — two to three weeks of normal play, no separate "audition" content. The bar is simply: do they show up when they say they will, and are they pleasant to be around in voice.
  3. One officer check-in at the end of the trial window, then a quick vote in #trial-check-in. No formal application form needed at this ambition level — a five-minute conversation covers it.
  4. Stop recruiting once you hit a stable 18–22. You don't need exactly twenty every night; you need enough consistent people that Hyjal Summit fills without scrambling, accounting for normal no-shows.

13 — Beyond the checklist

A few things worth doing that nobody asks for

A real guild identity

Pick a name, tabard colors, and a one-line motto before launch, not three weeks in. "La Fogata" and an ember-and-hearth color scheme is right there if you want it.

Mentorship pairing

When new recruits join, pair each with one core member as their first point of contact — cheaper than a formal onboarding doc and it actually builds the friendships that keep a guild alive.

A living roster sheet

One shared spreadsheet (or a pinned Discord post you update) tracking class, professions, and rough level — the low-tech version of the role tables above, kept current instead of static.

Monthly state-of-the-guild

A short, casual voice check-in once a month: what's working, what's annoying, does the schedule still fit. Fifteen minutes prevents most guild drama before it starts.

A Hardcore side pot

For anyone craving permadeath stakes, a completely separate, opt-in Hardcore character is a fun side project — it's fully isolated from your Normal-ruleset progress, so it never competes with main-guild time.

Watch the roadmap together

Treat the Spring and Summer 2027 update reveals as guild events — a shared watch or a Discord call when Blizzard publishes patch notes keeps the group's attention through the inevitable post-launch lull.
